# Avance × Custom Computer Creations × DadLAN × Josh — Fact-Checked Deep Research

**Research date:** 25 September 2026  
**Status:** Corrected replacement. The first version materially mischaracterised DadLAN by treating Josh's refurbished PC fleet as though it were DadLAN's purpose.

## Executive correction

DadLAN is **not a PC-refurbishment project or a hardware laboratory**.

DadLAN Ltd is an Australian not-for-profit charity whose constitutional purpose includes advancing mental health, social welfare and wellbeing, reducing loneliness and social isolation. It uses shared interests, especially gaming, technology, computing and family, to create social connection for men and dads. Its in-person programme operates through local chapters called **subnets**, with LAN gaming events supported by an online community.

Josh's refurbished laptops and LAN infrastructure are **enabling infrastructure for that social mission**. They lower the hardware/cost barrier to participation and help the events work.

## 1. DadLAN: what it actually is

DadLAN's website says its mission is to fight loneliness, improve mental-health outcomes, create community connections and provide dependable social spaces for men and dads.

Its constitution identifies **DadLAN LTD**, ABN **21 675 746 022**, as a not-for-profit public company limited by guarantee established as a charity. Objectives include reducing depression, anxiety and loneliness impacts, preventing social isolation, providing local physical events, supporting healthy dialogue for fathers and building community partnerships.

### Origin and model

DadLAN says it was established in 2023 around dads' shared interests in technology, computing, games and family.

ABC reported in August 2024 that founder William Parry started the first gathering after experiencing loneliness and noticing the lack of an equivalent for dads to the social connections his wife found through a mothers' group. Ten people initially met in a Newtown hall for a LAN party. The shared activity makes connection easier and can create space for deeper conversation.

## 8. Corrected intersection

The earlier model wrongly made DadLAN look like the bottom rung of an IT-career ladder. A better model has **people at the centre**.

### DadLAN
Goal: connection, belonging, wellbeing and an accessible shared activity.

Technology enables that:
- spare PCs remove cost barriers
- game deployment reduces setup friction
- routers/WireGuard connect networks/subnets
- documentation helps mixed-skill participants
- servers/launchers hide complexity
- volunteers troubleshoot so technical problems do not become social barriers

### Custom Computer Creations
Goal: make an individual's physical computer work well for its intended purpose.

Relevant expertise: diagnosis, builds, upgrades, repair, storage/data recovery, performance and customer explanation.

### Avance
Goal: make an organisation's technology secure, supportable, recoverable and aligned with business operations.

Relevant expertise: endpoint management, security, identity, monitoring, backup/DR, networks, documentation, asset lifecycle, vendor management and user support.

### Josh
The recurring role is better expressed as:

> **Using practical technology skills to remove friction for people.**

At DadLAN, the outcome is that a dad who may be isolated can sit down and join in.

With Brett, the outcome is a useful, reliable computer without unexpected costs.

At CCC-style bench level, broken or unsuitable hardware becomes functional and understandable.

At Avance, client technology becomes dependable enough for people to do their jobs.

## 9. Why Josh's old-PC work still matters

The fleet is still a valuable learning environment; it simply is not DadLAN's purpose.

It exposes Josh to multiple hardware generations, SSD migration, imaging/cloning, BIOS/boot issues, Windows deployment, BSODs, drivers, network shares, remote management, game compatibility, performance constraints and upgrade-value decisions.

Those skills can feed:
- DadLAN accessibility/support
- personal PC repair/handover
- endpoint troubleshooting at Avance

Transfer skills, not employer/client data, assets or tools without permission.

## 10. DadLAN-specific technical learning

High-value areas:
1. LAN design/troubleshooting: DHCP, addressing, switching, cabling and throughput.
2. WireGuard and routed multi-site LAN gaming.
3. Game-server hosting on Windows/Linux and Docker where relevant.
4. Old-game compatibility and patch/version management.
5. Rapid endpoint recovery and known-good images.
6. Fleet game deployment and local transfers.
7. Hardware triage for donated/spare PCs.
8. Event-day resilience: spares, cables, switches, displays, power and peripherals.
9. Accessible support for people who are there for community, not because they are PC enthusiasts.
10. Repeatable documentation so knowledge is shared across volunteers.

## 11. CCC-specific learning

Useful skills include systematic no-power/no-POST diagnosis, PSU/power testing, component fault isolation, SMART interpretation, cloning/data-safe migration, cooling/thermals, CPU/GPU/RAM stress testing, compatibility, upgrade economics, Windows/drivers, workmanship, customer intake/handover, cost approval, privacy/data handling and appropriate referral of specialist solder/board/data-recovery work.

Brett's tower is a useful live project across many of these areas.

## 12. Avance-specific learning

Useful areas include ticket triage/escalation, remote support, RMM/monitoring, patching, identity/MFA, security baselines, cloud administration, backup/restore, networking/Wi-Fi, asset/documentation discipline, PBX, business continuity and clear non-technical communication.

## 13. Strongest crossover example

A DadLAN spare laptop demonstrates the whole point:

**Donated machine → health/upgrade triage → SSD/RAM/imaging where worthwhile → game/network deployment → event testing → available to participant → social participation.**

The machine has achieved its purpose when it stops being the focus and lets the person participate.

## 15. Revised learning/media lens

The earlier Spotify research focused too heavily on PC building/repair. The actual intersection spans:

- **Community LAN/event infrastructure:** networking, game servers, WireGuard, Docker and event operations.
- **Refurbishment/repair:** old hardware, diagnostics, upgrades, thermals, testing, repair economics and reuse.
- **Custom PC/customer service:** build planning, compatibility, testing, quoting, communication and handover.
- **Managed IT:** MSP operations, endpoint management, security, backup, networking and documentation.

## 16. Final synthesis

The intersection is not:

**old PCs → custom PCs → MSP career**

It is:

**people need technology to stop getting in their way.**

DadLAN demonstrates that at community scale. A spare laptop, working network, WireGuard tunnel or custom launcher can be the difference between technical exclusion and joining other dads for games and connection.

CCC demonstrates it at individual computer/service scale: diagnose, build or repair the right machine, explain it and make it useful.

Brett's computer gives Josh practice with responsibility to a real recipient: budget, repair boundaries, testing, peripherals, backup and handover.

Avance demonstrates it at organisational scale: make technology secure, documented, monitored, backed up and dependable.

Josh's overlap is therefore **human-centred practical IT**: enough hardware, networking, software and support knowledge to make technology accessible and dependable while keeping the person's real goal central.

For DadLAN specifically, the goal is not the computer.

**The goal is connection.**
