# Avance × Custom Computer Creations × DadLAN × Josh — Deep Research

**Research date:** 25 September 2026  
**Scope:** Dubbo, NSW; Avance Business Technology / System State IT; Custom Computer Creations; DadLAN; Josh Parris's hands-on PC repair/build/refurbishment interests; Brett Sweeting PC handover; and the Spotify learning thread from this conversation.

## Executive finding

These are not four unrelated interests. They form a practical learning ecosystem around the lifecycle of small-business and personal computing.

- **DadLAN** is the low-risk laboratory: old and inexpensive PCs, SSD/RAM upgrades, imaging, Windows installs, hardware comparison, networking, compatibility, reuse, and keeping ageing machines useful.
- **Brett's PC and similar personal jobs** add a real-user/customer layer: budget constraints, parts sourcing, repair triage, testing, peripherals, backups, communication, handover and deciding when to refer specialist work.
- **Custom Computer Creations (CCC)** represents the local specialist bench/shop end of that continuum: custom PC/laptop builds, servicing, upgrades, data recovery, general repair and customer-focused support.
- **Avance Business Technology** represents the managed-business end: proactive support, endpoint/device management, cloud, identity, cyber security, backup/disaster recovery, networks, documentation, vendor management, PBX and development/integration.
- **Josh sits at the intersection**. The useful skill path is therefore broader than "learn to build gaming PCs": hardware + diagnosis + refurbishment + Windows + backup + networking + customer service + documentation + security + knowing when to escalate.

The most interesting bridge is that a single physical PC can pass through all four layers: acquire/refurbish it (DadLAN), build/repair/test it (CCC-like skills), deliver it to a real person (Brett/Parris Tech-like workflow), then manage it securely and reliably in an organisational environment (Avance-like discipline).

## 3. DadLAN as a hardware learning laboratory

DadLAN has evolved beyond simply "having old laptops for a LAN". Across the fleet work, the recurring activities have included:

- identifying exact models/specifications
- comparing CPUs, RAM, integrated/discrete graphics and storage
- deciding whether an old computer is worth upgrading
- installing SSDs and RAM
- cloning/imaging machines
- restoring Windows and dealing with boot/BIOS issues
- troubleshooting unstable systems and BSODs
- sourcing chargers and compatible parts
- distinguishing machines worth investing in from machines best left in a low-cost configuration
- networking multiple PCs for LAN use
- finding useful roles for hardware that would otherwise be obsolete/e-waste
- maintaining a mixed-generation fleet rather than one pristine modern gaming PC

The key educational advantage is **repetition with variety**. Building one modern PC teaches assembly. Maintaining a pile of mismatched old computers teaches diagnosis.

DadLAN also gives Josh something commercial bench work cannot: permission to experiment, fail, reinstall, swap parts and compare approaches without risking a customer's production device.

## 4. Brett Sweeting's PC as the bridge project

Brett's tower is a particularly good case study because it moves from hobbyist tinkering into a real handover workflow.

### Known tower configuration

- ASUS Crosshair V Formula-Z motherboard
- AMD FX-6300
- 16 GB RAM
- XFX Radeon R9 290 4 GB
- 240 GB SanDisk SSD
- 1 TB Seagate HDD

Brett has already paid **$100 for the tower**. Josh has already spent about **$70 on the SSD and PSU**, so further costs should not silently be absorbed without discussing them with Brett.

### Remaining handover work discussed

- install a Wi-Fi card
- take the tower to Custom Computer Creations for the case power-button solder/repair; CCC said they can do this free
- run Windows Update
- check SSD/HDD SMART health
- stress-test CPU and GPU and observe temperatures
- verify both monitor outputs
- establish proper backup
- check Avance for suitable spare monitors before buying online
- ask CCC whether it has suitable spare/cheap monitors
- ideally source two matching 21.5–24 inch 1080p displays with stands and DVI or compatible digital inputs
- if no suitable cheap local spares exist, the discussed fallback was **2 × Acer K222HQL 21.5-inch FHD 1920×1080 60 Hz VGA/DVI monitors for $115 total on eBay**
- before buying those, confirm both stands, both power cables and ideally two DVI cables are included
- the R9 290 can drive the proposed pair via its two DVI outputs, one display per port
- monitors are an **extra cost**, separate from the $100 tower price

### Why this project is educationally important

This is much closer to a professional workflow than merely assembling components:

**scope → budget → source → assemble/upgrade → diagnose → refer specialist repair → update → health-check → stress-test → peripheral verification → backup → handover.**

The missing step worth making explicit in future jobs is documentation: record the machine's specs, drive health, temperatures/test result, Windows/update state, backup arrangement, money spent and anything the recipient needs to know.

## 6. A four-layer model

| Layer | Environment | Core question | Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hardware | DadLAN | Can I make this machine useful? | parts, compatibility, SSD/RAM, imaging, BIOS, Windows, troubleshooting |
| 2. Bench/service | CCC-style work | Can I diagnose, repair, upgrade and test it properly? | systematic diagnosis, thermals, SMART, stress testing, physical repair boundaries |
| 3. Human handover | Brett / Parris Tech-style jobs | Is it right for this person and have I communicated the costs/risks? | scoping, budget, sourcing, explanation, backup, handover |
| 4. Managed IT | Avance | Can this endpoint stay secure, recoverable and supportable over time? | patching, RMM, identity, cyber security, backup/DR, networking, documentation, lifecycle |

The opportunity is to deliberately carry lessons **both directions**. Avance discipline can improve DadLAN/Brett work; repeated DadLAN hardware exposure can improve Josh's confidence diagnosing physical endpoints at work.

## 7. What to learn next

The highest-value topics around this intersection are:

1. PC architecture and component compatibility, including older generations.
2. A repeatable hardware diagnostic sequence: power, POST, memory, storage, thermals, GPU, drivers, OS.
3. SMART interpretation and storage failure triage.
4. CPU/GPU/RAM stress testing and knowing what constitutes a meaningful failure.
5. Cooling, airflow, thermal paste and ageing hardware.
6. Windows deployment, drivers, updates, recovery and imaging.
7. Backup design and restore testing.
8. Home/small-office networking and Wi-Fi.
9. Refurbishment economics: when an SSD/RAM/PSU/GPU upgrade is sensible and when to stop spending.
10. Customer intake, scope, consent, cost approval and handover documentation.
11. Data handling/privacy when working on somebody else's machine.
12. Security baseline: supported OS, patching, MFA/account hygiene, endpoint protection.
13. Escalation/referral: soldering/board repair, difficult data recovery and other specialist work.
14. E-waste, reuse and right-to-repair thinking.
15. MSP concepts: RMM, asset inventory, documentation, monitoring and lifecycle management.

## 9. Recommended listening structure

Rather than following only one show, rotate by the job in front of you:

- **When working on DadLAN:** repair/refurbishment + old hardware + low-budget sysadmin.
- **When working on Brett-style builds:** custom-PC/build + diagnostics + testing + customer handover.
- **For Avance growth:** sysadmin/MSP + security + backup + networking + endpoint management.
- **For Parris Tech:** repair-shop operations + customer communication + quoting/cost boundaries + data/privacy.

This keeps the learning grounded in real machines rather than accumulating abstract PC trivia.

## 10. Practical workflow to standardise

A reusable checklist for future machines:

**Intake → identify → inspect → baseline → agree budget → back up → repair/upgrade → OS/drivers/updates → SMART → memory/CPU/GPU tests → thermals → network/Wi-Fi → ports/displays/audio → security baseline → backup/restore plan → clean → document → explain → hand over.**

For a customer/friend machine, add explicit approval before extra spending and explicit consent before handling personal data.

For an organisational endpoint, add asset documentation, identity/access, RMM/monitoring, policy/security controls and business continuity.

## 11. Boundaries between the environments

The overlap is valuable, but the environments should not be blurred.

- DadLAN is personal experimentation and community/hobby infrastructure.
- Brett's PC is a real person's machine with real money and expectations.
- Parris Tech is Josh's own service context.
- CCC is an independent local business and specialist.
- Avance is Josh's employer and its equipment, clients, processes and commercial information belong in that professional context.

Skills can transfer between them. Client data, employer data, licences, tools, stock, credentials and commercial arrangements should not transfer casually between them.

The Brett monitor plan illustrates the right principle: it is reasonable to **ask** whether Avance has spare suitable equipment, but ownership/permission and any cost must be explicit rather than assumed.

## 12. Deep-research conclusion

The common thread is **computer lifecycle stewardship**.

Josh is building experience across:
- acquisition and reuse
- hardware assessment
- upgrading
- repair
- operating-system setup
- stability testing
- networking
- security
- backup
- user education
- cost control
- escalation
- long-term management

That is broader and more useful than simply becoming "good at building PCs".

DadLAN supplies volume and experimentation. Brett-style projects supply accountability to an individual. CCC provides a local example and specialist escalation path for physical PC work. Avance supplies professional managed-IT discipline and exposure to what happens after a computer leaves the bench.

The strongest development direction is therefore to become unusually good at the **whole journey from neglected hardware to dependable endpoint**, while maintaining clear boundaries between hobby, personal service, specialist referral and employer work.
