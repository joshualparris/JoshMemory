# Narromine: research dossier and plan to make it drivable from Repository Town

Status: Phase 1 rebuilt from the real map (OpenStreetMap). Branch `claude/narromine`.
Every fact below carries its source. Where sources disagree the disagreement is recorded, not
resolved; check the primary source (ABS, council, AWM, Trove) before putting a number on screen.

## 1. Why Narromine, and where it sits

- Narromine is about **40 km (≈30 min) west of Dubbo along the A32 Mitchell Highway**, which
  largely follows the Macquarie River ([australiatravelquestions](https://australiatravelquestions.com/?p=41997),
  [Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Narromine)).
- The Dubbo–Narromine stretch was recently widened with overtaking lanes in a $32 million
  safety upgrade ([Western Plains App](https://westernplainsapp.com.au/NewsStory/travel-between-dubbo-and-narromine-gets-safer/67246cf40e3bfa002e445e6b)).
- Repository Town is a Dubbo-flavoured town (Dubbo radio, NSW signs, Central West farms), so a
  drive west to Narromine is the natural first "other town".

## 3. Country and First Nations

- Wiradjuri Country. The name comes from a pastoral run whose name is a Wiradjuri word; sources
  differ on meaning: *ngarru-mayiny* "honey people" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narromine)),
  "place of many lizards" or "place of honey" ([Aussietowns](https://aussietowns.com.au/?p=11175)).
  Show both meanings and say they differ.
- The land lay within **Wangaibon** territory; in December 2021 NSW restored the name **Wambuul**
  ("winding river") for the Macquarie River ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narromine)).
- Rule for the game: First Nations content comes only from published Aboriginal-authored
  public sources; no sacred or sensitive sites; no invented Aboriginal characters or stories.
  Nobody is to be contacted for guidance (Josh's decision); anything uncertain stays out.

## 6. Economy and land (drives farms, trucks and jobs)

- Crops: wheat, barley, oats, canola, soy, lucerne, dryland and irrigated cotton; stone fruit and
  citrus. Irrigation by flood, pivot and linear, from bores, river and on-farm storage; Burrendong
  Dam underpins it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narromine), [The Land](https://www.theland.com.au/story/6863008/cows-give-way-to-crops/), [FarmBuy listing](https://farmbuy.com/570-dulla-dulla-road-narromine-nsw-2821-353243)).
- Mining: Alkane's Tomingley gold operation in the shire; extension to 2032 (San Antonio, Roswell);
  ~18,400 oz and $62.3 m revenue in the Sept-2024 quarter ([NSW Planning](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/major-projects/projects/tomingley-gold-extension-project-0), [ASX](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20240624/pdf/064vq97y0xlm8g.pdf)).
  "200 local jobs" appears only on a low-quality site; do not use.
- Floods: the town has flood-liable parts; formal studies exist ([Narromine Flood Study](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/nsw-fdp-narromine-flood-study-report/resource/54c8a5c2-7bc6-4474-ab39-15dc0fe7db52), [Narromine–Oxley floodplain study](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/51658338-0aa1-4fdc-8853-f468ed12558c)).

## 7. People rule

Same rule as the market stallholders: **no living private people**. Named characters only where
they are long-dead and well documented (Trove, ADB/Obituaries Australia, AWM). Living public
figures (e.g. a sportsperson with a statue) appear only as the statue/plaque with verified facts,
never as talking NPCs. Narromine residents in the game are otherwise fictional, unnamed by real
family names taken from local records.

## 8. How to make Narromine drivable — the plan

### Scale
Real distance 40 km. At game speed that is 20+ minutes of empty highway. Compress the drive to
**about 2.5 km of road (≈90 s at 100 km/h)** with signs saying the real 40 km, and fade/fast-travel
option for impatient players. Narromine itself is built near real scale.

### World placement
- Put Narromine in its **own coordinate region far to the west** (for example centred at
  x = −6000) so it never overlaps Repo Town lots and float precision stays fine.
- The **Mitchell Highway** leaves Repo Town's western edge as a new driveable road strip. Today
  `roadAllows()` in `src/world/player-driving.js` (via `onPlannedOrCoreRoad`) bounds driving to
  the town's drawn roads, so the highway must be added as a real road record, not just a mesh.
- **Streaming:** build Narromine's meshes only when the player is within ~600 m of it, and drop
  Repo Town's distant detail while there (the same near/far pattern the resident models use), so
  phones do not hold both towns in memory.

### Highway content (cheap, high impact)
NSW signage (reuse `road-signs.js`: 100 km/h, "Narromine 40", "Trangie 70"), paddocks of wheat
and cotton, irrigation pivots, the river line of red gums on the left, the railway alongside, a
truck or two, overtaking lanes matching the 2024 upgrade, and the car radio continuing from Dubbo.

### Simulation
Narromine gets its own small `TownLife`-style schedule (a few dozen residents, shops with real
opening hours, trains on the Main West line) but shares the clock, weather and the player's
wallet. Keep it deterministic like Repo Town so saves restore exactly.

### Phases (one PR each, browser-tested)
1. **Highway + arrival.** Driveable Mitchell Highway west, signs, paddocks, river, a
   "Welcome to Narromine" sign, and Dandaloo Street facades (Royal Hotel, Narromine Hotel,
   Soldiers' Wall). Search/teleport "Narromine". Test: drive from Repo Town to Dandaloo St
   without leaving the road check; no page errors; phone frame time.
2. **Aerodrome + Aviation Museum.** Hangars, the museum interior with the verified exhibits,
   the RAAF memorial wall, a fillable "museum collection" quest.
3. **Glider flight.** Weekend glider joy-flight minigame from the aerodrome.
4. **River and civic block.** Rotary Park walk, Court House museum, visitor centre, station.
5. **Residents and life.** Small resident roster, shop hours, trains, farm trucks, flood
   history as an optional weather event (1926/1990 plaques).
6. **First Nations content** only from published Aboriginal-authored sources (no outreach).

### Decisions (Josh, 2026-11-03)
- The compressed ~90 s drive is right.
- **Never contact anyone on Josh's behalf.** No emails, calls or messages to the council, the
  Local History Room, the Local Aboriginal Land Council or anyone else. First Nations content is
  limited to published, Aboriginal-authored public sources; anything that would need community
  guidance stays out of the game.

### Phase 1 — built
- `src/world/narromine-layout.js` (pure data: highway, region, streets, buildings, fences),
  `src/world/narromine.js` (drawing; the corridor is always drawn, the town only west of x -1300),
  driving on the highway/streets with a 100 km/h open-highway limit, a gap in the town's west
  wall, E-prompt facts at each building and the Soldiers' Wall, search "Narromine".
- Test `tests/narromine-browser.mjs`: drives a sedan from Street 4 to Dandaloo Street in 99 s
  of game time at up to 100 km/h, never off a driveable road; town hidden at home, shown on
  arrival; Royal Hotel fact; search teleport; no page errors.
- Open question: should Trangie and Tomingley follow later in the same way?

### Heights from shadows (tried, not adopted)
The imagery was flown on 23 July 2012. Pole shadows put the sun at 9.7° east of north and 37.1° above the horizon, so 1.32 m of shadow = 1 m of height. This is reliable only where a wall shadows open, light ground. The Royal's front block came out at 4.0 m, matching the model. On the crowded main street, most shadows fall on other roofs, verandahs, dark bitumen or trees, so the method is not used to set heights automatically.

## Building heights from airborne LiDAR (NSW Spatial Services, CC BY 4.0)

Josh ordered the surveys through ELVIS (Geoscience Australia) on 4 Nov 2026:
- **MacquarieFloodplainSouth 2023:** tiles 55_6166432 and 55_6166433, 4 pts/m², GDA2020 MGA55 + AHD, about 11.4 million points.
- **Narromine 2011:** tile 6166432, GDA94 MGA55. Used only where the 2023 survey does not reach.

The raw LAZ files are not committed (about 160 MB). They are kept in Josh's Google Drive.

**Method.** `tools/narromine_lidar.py` takes each building polygon and measures:
- the surveyor-classified building points (class 6) inside it, and the ground points (class 2) within 8 m;
- ground = median ground elevation;
- heights above ground: eave = 5th percentile, ridge = 98th percentile, typical = median.

Results go into `src/world/narromine-lidar-data.js`, keyed to every OSM building and to every detailed part and verandah.

**Effect in game**
- **Mapped buildings:** extruded to their measured median roof height wherever surveyed. 77 are measured; the others keep their tag-based guesses.
- **Detailed parts:** wall height and roof pitch from the measurements. For a pitched roof, eave = (median − 0.3 × ridge) / 0.7.
- **Upper-floor features:** stretched to the new wall height.
- **Verandahs:** their measured height is used only when the survey sees them clearly. Awnings under a roof overhang, like the Post Office's, read as the roof and are ignored.

**Checks against earlier estimates**

| Building | Earlier estimate | LiDAR |
|---|---|---|
| Royal front | 4.2 m | ≈ 4.3 m eave, 6.4 m ridge |
| Royal verandah | 3.25 m | 2.8 m |
| Narromine Hotel | 7.6 m | ≈ 9.5 m eave, 11 m ridge |
| Narromine Hotel verandah | 7.0 m | 7.1–7.5 m |
| Post Office | 8.4 m | ≈ 9.7 m, near-flat roof at 10.4 m |
| Coles halls | 7.1 m | ≈ 5.5 m eave, 7.5–7.7 m ridge |
| Coles awning | 3.25 m | 3.3 m |

**Limits**
- Parts under about 15 m² (the Royal's lean-to) have too few points and keep their traced heights.
- Where traced parts overlap a taller neighbour, the neighbour's roof can lift the percentiles.
- Facades, colours and materials still come from photos; LiDAR gives only heights and roof shape.

### Measured geometry (`src/world/narromine-hotel.js`)
- **Cadastre & street alignment:** Anchored to the NSW Spatial Services cadastre street corners of Lot 13 DP519453 (`SE = [-4819.98, -707.11]`, `NE = [-4815.95, -734.04]`, Dandaloo Street frontage `27.232 m` at bearing `8.49°` E of N, perpendicular to Derribong Avenue).
- **2012 aerial parallax correction:** The 2012 NSW aerial image has oblique camera lean that shifts the `11 m` hotel roof ~`3.5 m` east of the ground-level cadastre boundary, which previously caused the three traced two-storey wings (`Derribong Avenue wing`, `Dandaloo Street wing`, `north wing`) to render at mismatched yaw angles (`-3.4°`, `-5.8°`, `-9.4°`), stepped eave heights (`8.83 m`, `9.47 m`, `9.89 m`), and an exaggerated `8.1 m` verandah depth. The dedicated builder aligns the two-storey U-shaped masonry wall planes directly to the cadastre street boundaries with a `45°` chamfered SE corner (`1.65 m` splay).
- **2023 NSW LiDAR heights:** Uniform main wall eave at `8.90 m`, continuous corrugated-iron hipped roof ridge at `10.95 m` (`11.08 m` SE corner parapet apex), two-storey wraparound verandah deck at `3.75 m`, verandah roof eaves at `7.18 m` rising to `7.68 m` at the wall ledger, and footpath verandah depth `3.55 m`.
- **Chimneys:** Four tall red-brick chimneys (`#9b4b38`) with vertical pilaster ribs, cream-rendered necking bands, 3-course stepped corbelled brick heads, and twin cylindrical terracotta pots at their aerial- and Street View-verified coordinates (`s=5.35, w=2.35`, `s=20.65, w=2.45`, `s=7.45, w=13.50`, `s=22.80, w=14.20`).

### Photographically verified architectural details
- **Walls, plinth & exposed north brickwork:** Smooth cream-rendered street walls (`#e6dac2`) above a `1.05 m` warm grey-brown painted masonry plinth (`#867e74`) with projecting stone cap, mid-level stringcourse, and moulded eaves cornice. As verified in Street View `102255.014.jpg`, the upper north-facing wall (`s = 27.23 m`, `y = 3.85..8.90 m`) above the adjoining single-storey northern shopfront is unpainted exposed red face-brickwork (`#9b4b38`) with three recessed upper sash windows.
- **Curved & stepped SE corner parapet:** Extruded 3D pediment across the `45°` SE corner featuring a semicircular central arch (`y = 11.08 m`) with raised 3D archivolt hood moulding, recessed sunburst/fan tympanum, `1901` and `NARROMINE HOTEL` serif lettering, stepped consoles/shoulders, flanking pilaster piers with moulded capitals and finials, and East/South return parapet wings along the eaves to secondary stepped piers with orb finials.
- **Two-storey wraparound verandah & open cast-iron lacework:** `7` bays along Dandaloo Street + `1` splayed `45°` SE corner bay + `6` bays along Derribong Avenue + North/West end returns, supported by `14` heritage-green fluted cast-iron columns (`#2c4c32`) with moulded base plinths, collars, and capitals. Both upper and lower verandah levels carry open-filigree cream Victorian cast-iron lacework valances, 3D stepped corner brackets, and upper balcony balustrade panels (`#efe8d6`, rendered with alpha-cutout procedural textures matching the photographed geometric ring-band, vertical baluster, and leafy scrollwork pattern).
- **Bay-by-bay windows, doors & wall fixtures:**
  - *Dandaloo Street upper floor (Bays 1–7):* Recessed sash windows (`0.95 × 1.65 m`) with projecting sills, architraves, and glazing bars; window box air-conditioner in Bay 1; wider 3-light sash window (`1.55 × 1.65 m`) in Bay 3; wall-mounted white split-system air-conditioner unit (`0.82 × 0.32 m`) north of Bay 7 (`102253.251.jpg`).
  - *Dandaloo Street ground floor (Bays 1–7) & SE corner:* Recessed timber pub doorway with glazed fanlight and flanking green `TAB` signs at the `45°` SE corner; double panelled timber pub doors in Bay 3 (main bar entry) and Bay 4 (`LOCAL LIQUOR` bottle-shop entry); single timber door in Bay 6; recessed ground-floor bar sash windows in Bays 1, 2, 5, and 7; framed gold/dark wall plaques and notices between bays.
  - *Derribong Avenue facade (Bays S1–S6):* Six upper sash windows and six ground-floor openings including four arched-head ground-floor pub windows with rendered arch rings/keystones, recessed timber doorways in Bays S2 and S4, white wall-mounted pub noticeboards, and two yellow-and-red kerbside bollards (`102238.751.jpg`).
- **Signage & street furniture:** `NARROMINE HOTEL MOTEL` fascia sign at the `45°` SE balcony fascia (`#203824` with cream border and gold sub-caption), two-sided `Narromine Hotel` projecting upper corner blade sign with gold arched crest (`#7a1f24`), red `LOCAL LIQUOR` sign (`#c42026`) over Bay 4, green `TAB` signs (`#1c6b36`) at the SE corner, SE kerbside green/blue directional road fingerboards (`HOSPITAL -> THE WATERING WHARF / Tullamore`, `TOWN CENTRE -> Dubbo / Nyngan`), corner timber utility pole, and two street trees in timber guards.
- **Adjoining northern shopfront (`s = 27.23..41.20 m`):** Single-storey dark-charcoal brick/clad shopfront (`#2d3034`) with glazed aluminium-framed display windows, central recessed doorway with bright magenta/pink surround (`#d4487a`), stepped white lattice parapet screen, and corrugated gable roof (`102253.251.jpg`–`102255.014.jpg`).

### Recorded uncertainties & limitations
- **Rear courtyard & western outbuildings:** The rear infill (`5.8 m`), west building (`5.4 m`), west annex (`4.0 m`), and north-west outbuilding (`3.9 m`) inside the lot are not visible from the Dandaloo Street or Derribong Avenue Street View frames; they are modelled from their 2023 NSW LiDAR heights and 2012 aerial footprints with assumed cream-rendered/weatherboard walls and corrugated roofs.
- **Cast-iron lacework pattern:** The open Victorian cast-iron lacework valence, brackets, and balustrade panels reproduce the photographed motif (top geometric ring frieze, vertical baluster grilles, and curved rinceau scrollwork) procedurally rather than from a foundry casting blueprint.
- **Interior:** The Narromine Hotel is currently an exterior architectural model with sliced perimeter wall colliders so the wraparound verandah footpaths along both Dandaloo Street and Derribong Avenue are fully walkable; interior rooms are not yet modelled.
