# Repository Town — government and civic delivery audit
**Date:** 19 October 2026 (Australia/Sydney)  
**Scope:** canonical `repository-town/` on `main`, plus Render realism branch `feat/town-realism-power-network-20261019`. Audit of source behaviour, not a claimed desktop or Android playtest.  
**Finding:** There is a real running town economy and public-service simulation, but **no functioning representative council or municipal decision-and-delivery workflow**.

## Evidence and current architecture
- `src/world/life.js`: `TownLife`, `CANDIDATES`, `JOBS`, `VILLAGER_NAMES`, `newDay`, `election`, `vote`, `sale`, `repair`, `repairPowerLine`, `utilitiesHour`, `pay`, `ledger`, `snapshot`, `restore`.
- `src/ui/townpanels.js`: `hallPanel`, `civicPanel`, `ledgerPanel`. The Town Hall's menu provides an instant repair, power-fault demonstration and three candidate voting buttons.
- `src/world/townlife.js`: physical high-street public buildings and workers. Real facilities appear, but their existence does not itself constitute administrative authority.
- `tests/town-simulation.mjs`: verifies treasury, election occurrence, repairs, utilities, 14 simulated days. Does not test councillor elections, approvals, mayoral authority or work-order fulfilment.
- `docs/LIVING_TOWN_MASTERPLAN.md`: notes much fuller government institutions as planned candidates, not delivered functionality.

## Direct answers
### How is the mayor elected?
- `CANDIDATES = ['Vern','Rowan','Sage']`; initial mayor Vern.
- First election on town day 7, then every seven days.
- Players can cast one vote **per day**, not once per election: `civic.voted` is reset in `newDay()`. Multiple ballots in the same election cycle can therefore be generated by one player.
- Other residents do **not** cast tracked individual votes. Each candidate receives a pseudorandom base 6–14 votes; the incumbent receives a +2 advantage. Add the player's accumulated ballots, choose the largest total, break ties in candidate-list order.
- Winner updates `civic.mayor`; no platform, budget authority, policies, promises, councillor seats or resident approval consequences change.
- NPC work roster has a fixed single `mayor` job assigned to the 22nd villager, Vern, regardless of election outcome. Rowan serves as town guide in other scenes; neither Rowan nor Sage becomes the actual Town Hall staff representative on winning.

### Who sits on the council/board?
**Nobody has been appointed.** No data model for councillors, deputy mayor, general manager, council clerk, elected wards, committees, portfolios, opposition or meeting roles. The `civic` object stores only a current mayor string and finances/services. Do not invent current councillor names.

### What power does the mayor have?
No meaningful policy or administrative authority is attached to office: no decision interface for the mayor, meeting chair, motions, votes, directions to an executive, approved budget or enforceable public policy. The player, who need not be mayor, can trigger repair actions through Town Hall and can even generate a synthetic west-feeder fault. The tax rate is initialised at 10%, with no normal policy mechanism for elected officials to change it.

### How does a public job get done today?
- Service condition drops a little each new day. Utilities respond automatically on the hour to fuel/power/water/worker status.
- The player presses `Repair the [most-worn] network`. `repair(service)` validates a service name, treasury >= $90 and Town Hall stock >= 2 parts boxes, instantly deducts cash/parts and adds 35 condition points. It logs that council repaired the service.
- `repairPowerLine(line)` similarly charges $90 + two parts and immediately removes a failed feeder from the model.
- **Missing between button and outcome:** reports/requests, approvals, work orders, job assignment, procurement, equipment, route planning, on-site activity, elapsed work time, inspection, signed completion and evidence of condition change.
- Existing work and services elsewhere are substantive: scheduled NPC shifts, farm/mill/bakery throughput, stock-based freight, electricity generation/fuel, water, wastewater, bins, recycling/foundry, post and retail. These are predominantly clock/rule-driven rather than decisions made by local government officers.

## Government finance — actually implemented
- Opening treasury $4,800.
- Daily household rates: up to $15 debited from every simulated villager wallet (not from all 188 buildings); daily $900 grant credited without application.
- 10% fixed sales tax; 35% of private business daily surplus over a retained $50 float goes to the treasury. Public premises remit takings; labour costs are paid by employers or topped up from treasury. Public services, spare parts and freight fuel/ore imports consume treasury money.
- Strong foundation: money/stock balances and repeatable 14-day economy tests. Weak oversight: no adopted budget, deficit controls or spending priorities, audited annual accounts, procurement rules, delegated authorisations, public tendering or recorded councillor decisions.
- Civic health indicator has no genuine government service feedback; happiness is simplified into power/water status. Neither influences individual ballots.

## Proposed governance model (not implemented)
Use an NSW-inspired *small-town* system rather than a giant municipal bureaucracy:
1. Elected governing body: either councillor-elected mayor (two town-years in compressed time) or popularly elected mayor (four town-years), plus a small elected council; choose and document one coherent franchise and ward model. Do not conflate the elected body with employees.
2. Named, persistent councillors elected from **actual town residents** with home address, background, priority preferences, term, attendance, declarations of interest and votes. Persist elections and their detailed totals.
3. Mayor chairs meetings, represents community and provides leadership, but does not unilaterally create work. When leadership changes, reassign the real NPC job and interactions. Name/roles are bound to the actual officeholder.
4. General manager (council-appointed paid staff) runs departments: finance/admin; public works/roads; electricity/water/wastewater; waste/recycling; planning; community/emergency liaison. Public employees answer through their manager, not individual councillors.
5. Public agenda and motions: residents submit problems, staff investigate and cost options, mayor sets agenda, councillors debate/vote; publish minutes, resolutions, dissent and conflict declarations. Delegated routine jobs may be authorised by the GM; major expenses require council resolution.
6. Adopted budget with capital/operational line items, recurrent wages, grants, tax rates, capital projects and restricted funding. Track forecast vs actual. Do not mint money without logged source.
7. **Physical service delivery:** report -> triage/priority -> inspector visit -> estimate and stock check -> delegated approval or council vote -> purchase/allocate materials -> named crew + vehicle -> journey -> time-bound work -> inspection -> measurable service recovery -> invoice and auditable closeout. Fail gracefully when workers, fuel, roads or funds are unavailable.
8. Respect consequences: rates/taxes/land-use/service levels feed household finances, happiness and beliefs, thus subsequent votes. Not all residents have the same preferences.
9. Persistence: election register, councillors, resolutions, budgets, work orders, contractors, event log and audit evidence must survive reload and migration. Keep the private/public privacy gate intact.
10. Town Hall should contain an actual Council Chamber, public gallery, mayor's office, clerk/GM desk, service counter, works dispatch; meaningful interactions rather than floating labels alone.

## One end-to-end acceptance example
Water main fails -> household reports no water -> Water Works reports the affected supply zone -> town clerk opens a tracked case -> worker attends site and establishes cause, parts, labour, risk and deadline -> GM checks authority/budget; if above delegation, council debates and votes in a scheduled meeting -> treasury reserves money and parts -> works manager assigns a named team and truck -> route and scheduled labour consume time, materials and pay -> water pressure/service remains impaired until inspection -> on completion, finances and service are reconciled and event/meeting/work logs show exactly **who decided, who performed the work, when, for how much and why**. A failed vote, lack of funds or missing technician must leave a visible unresolved case, not silently repair the network.

## Tests to add before claiming realism
- Player can vote only once per election and ballots remain one-per-elector across save/reload.
- Election result equals a count of actual eligible resident votes; fixed seed reproduces result; winner's NPC identity and work assignment update immediately.
- Elected councillors and mayor are all real existing people with unique persistent IDs and mandates.
- Council motion cannot execute without quorum/required majority; conflict-of-interest votes cannot count.
- Delegate authorisation and council authorisation reject out-of-budget/over-limit requests.
- Work order transitions and physical crew check are mandatory before `services[network]` improves.
- Material, wage, fuel and vehicle inventories conserve assets across an entire work order; abandoned work remains incomplete.
- Public council register, posted decisions and event history survive save/load.
- Existing full 14-day economy, power-grid, traffic, privacy and farmhouse tests continue passing.

## NSW reference model
- NSW Electoral Commission: `https://elections.nsw.gov.au/elections/how-voting-works/voting-in-new-south-wales/how-does-a-local-government-election-work`. NSW councils use either direct election of mayor or election by councillors, and councillors have their own elections; civic elections are not one random round every seven days.
- NSW Office of Local Government, Councillor Handbook: `https://www.olg.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/councillor-handbook.pdf`. The governing body makes strategic decisions; general manager implements them.
- Local Government Act 1993 (NSW), sections 226, 227, 335 and 377 for the mayor, election, general manager and delegations.

**Outcome of audit:** Documentation only; no council/governance system has been added or represented as already working.
