# The Undercity — Project Bible (authoring edition)
> **Status:** Existing repository lore takes precedence. Where this bible suggests unrevealed mysteries, they are *proposed narrative options*, not proof of established canon.

## Premise
Beneath the stone roof, the city is lit by cultivated moon-moss, vulnerable lanterns and a false sky that somebody maintains at a considerable human cost. You escape the Bell-Cage and find a city ruled through paperwork, delicate threats and memory.

Lantern Court, the Inkward, the Siltway quays, Glass Arcade, Moss Choir, Sootworks, Forgotten Stairs and Listening Edge each show a different facet of the same authority. Five factions have incompatible visions of survival. A clockmaker's impossible appointment draws you into the wider investigation.

The personal ambitions ask about the Debt of the Moth King, the river's Unfinished Testament, the Child Behind the Glass and the Council's Last Fire. Each is a 32-chapter arc. The player's past, the fate of witnesses and the public record should change dialogue and endings over time.

## Ten rules of tone
1. Second person and present tense for the player.
2. Make every uncanny scene materially specific.
3. Gothic, wistful, politically alert; never merely grim.
4. Use dry humour and weary bureaucrats sparingly.
5. Reveal motives through action and distinct dialogue.
6. Never flatter the player.
7. No consequence-free grand victory.
8. Speak of institutions without making every clerk identical.
9. Record rumours separately from canon.
10. The mysteries must sustain human stakes beneath the spectacle.

## Dispositions
Current game qualities include opposing tendencies such as generous/ruthless, honest/devious and steadfast/fickle. Refer to the **actual** `content/world.js` registry for the full authoritative quirk set and CP opposition; never introduce a new quirk from prose alone.

## Five factions
- **Velvet Ledger**: brokers of secrets and leverage; offer credit, documents and dangerous introductions.
- **Ash Wardens**: roof defenders; offer equipment, guards and protective oaths.
- **Thorn Assembly**: artists and civic dissenters; offer testimony, performances and public pressure.
- **Copper Hands**: scavengers, smugglers and makers; offer repair, routes and materials.
- **Dawnless Collective**: reformers seeking structural political change; offer organisation, petitions and shared resources.

## The central mystery
The Council's official version of the false sky is incomplete. Existing ambition content implicates stolen names, forbidden experiments and unpaid promises. **Proposed unifying possibility:** each system was originally called temporary and became permanent through a succession of convenient silences. Do not disclose any single resolution outside the approved act.
